# Discovery and growth playbook

## Audience
Primary: data engineers, software engineers, AI/ML engineers, architects and CS/engineering students. Secondary: technical PMs, engineering leaders, founders and curious professionals. Seek qualified reach, not random virality.

## Search packaging
Build each episode around a phrase people actually search: “OLTP vs OLAP”, “What is RAG?”, “ETL vs ELT”, “CAP theorem explained”. Use it naturally in title, opening narration, on-screen label and first description lines. Avoid keyword stuffing.

## Hook patterns
- Question: “Why can't one database do everything?”
- Contrast: “OLTP and OLAP both use data—but solve different problems.”
- Pain: “Why can analytics queries slow down an application database?”
- Analogy: “Think of OLTP as the cashier and OLAP as the analyst.”
- Myth: “More scalable doesn't automatically mean better.”

## CTA library
Choose one: “Save this for your next system-design interview.” / “Share this with someone learning data engineering.” / “Follow for more complex tech, simply drawn.” / “Next: [related concept].”

## Hashtags
Hashtags support topic classification; they are not a growth hack. Keep `#DataDoodleTech` consistent and add only relevant topic tags.
- General: `#DataEngineering #DataArchitecture #SystemDesign`
- Databases: `#Database #SQL #DatabaseDesign`
- AI/RAG: `#GenerativeAI #RAG #LLM`
- Distributed systems: `#DistributedSystems #SystemDesign #SoftwareArchitecture`

## Distribution
Publish natively with tailored title/caption/cover. Avoid third-party watermarks. Share to Stories when useful. Participate in relevant communities only when self-promotion is permitted and the post genuinely helps. Adapt selected videos into LinkedIn carousels. Collaborate with educators/engineers whose audiences overlap. Turn recurring questions into episodes.

## Cadence
Start sustainably, e.g. 3 quality videos/week. Consistency is a production discipline, not a guarantee of reach. Reassess after 20–30 posts.

## Analytics loop
Capture at 48 hours and 7 days: reach/views, viewed-vs-swiped, average view duration, average percentage viewed, shares, saves, comments, profile visits, follows/subscribers, traffic sources and search terms.
- Low viewed rate: improve hook/first frame.
- Good start, weak retention: simplify setup and improve visual progression.
- Strong retention, weak follows: clarify brand promise and series continuity.
- Strong saves/shares: create sequels.
- High views but irrelevant audience: refine topic targeting.
Change one variable at a time when testing.
